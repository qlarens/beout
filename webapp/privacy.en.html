Be. Out! — terms of use and privacy policy

Revision 4 of 24 November 2026 · Русский · Українська · English

Be. Out! is a free, non-commercial workout tracker (cycling, running, walking) in Telegram. By tapping “I accept” in the chat with the bot, you agree to these terms and consent to the data processing described below. Without your consent the bot records nothing.

1. Who processes the data

The owner of the bot (the operator). Contact: github.com/qlarens/beout/issues. This is a public page, so don't post personal data there. You can delete and export your data yourself with bot commands (see section 6).

2. What data is stored

  • Location while you share it live: coordinates, time and accuracy of each point. Only while you share your live location with the bot yourself.
  • Telegram ID, the chat ID and the ID of the live location message — to link points to a workout and to reply to you.
  • Weight, if you enter it (otherwise 75 kg is used) — to estimate calories.
  • Workout stats calculated from the points: type, distance, time, speed, calories, records.
  • Language: the language of your Telegram (Russian, Ukrainian or English; any other counts as English) and the language chosen in the Mini App profile — so that the bot, the app and the cards speak it.
  • Consent record: date and version of the accepted terms, the date of your first consent (sign-up) and when you last opened the Mini App, to the nearest 10 minutes.
  • Joint workouts and groups: joint sessions you created or joined; which groups you're on the leaderboard of and post workouts to, your replies to meetups (“Going”, “Maybe”, “Can't”), meetups and challenges you created, duels — only after you tap the button or send the command yourself.
  • Your phone's time zone — when you open the Mini App, to show meetup times and the “early bird” award in your local time.
  • Emergency contacts: Telegram IDs of people who agreed via your link themselves. If you agreed to be someone's contact, only your Telegram ID and language (so that an SOS arrives in it) are stored.
  • Invitations: if you first came via an “Invite friends” link, the Telegram ID of the person who invited you and the date. A random code is stored for your own link.
  • Card photo, if you add one as a background yourself: it is processed in the server's memory only to draw the card and is never stored. The finished image goes to the chat with the bot or, if you tap “Save”, is available via a random link for 10 minutes.
  • Anonymised counters per day: how many cards were sent, how many times /start was tapped before consent; no Telegram IDs.
  • Elevation gain of a workout, if it could be determined (see Open-Meteo in section 4).

Your name, username, phone number and profile photo are not stored. The bot requests your name from Telegram when showing it: to joint workout participants, on a group leaderboard, in the list of invited friends and in the operator's statistics. No cookies or trackers are used.

2a. What others see

  • By default only you see your workouts.
  • Workout card (route on a map, distance, time, speed, calories; the Instagram card also has the date and elevation gain) reaches a chat or a social network only when you send it yourself. The map shows where you started and finished — keep that in mind if you start from home.
  • Joint workout: session participants see your name, route and stats of workouts started within 12 hours after the session was created. Invitations go by a link that a participant shares.
  • Group leaderboard: group members see your name, weekly and monthly distance by type and your streak of days with workouts — only after “Join”; “Leave” removes you from the leaderboard. While you're on it, your kilometres also count towards the group's challenges and club battles, and the weekly results may include awards: longest workout, the start time of the earliest one (“early bird”), number of days with workouts, growth vs last week. In a club battle the other group only sees your group's name and its total distance.
  • Group feed: if you tap “Post my workouts”, group members will see a card after each workout (route on the map, distance, time, speed) with your name. The map shows where you started and finished. “Stop posting” turns it off.
  • Meetups and duels: group members see the names of those who replied “Going” or “Maybe”, and after the meetup the distances of those who joined the joint workout. In a duel you started or accepted, the group sees both your distances for the duel's period.
  • SOS: when you press SOS and confirm, your emergency contacts receive your name and last location.
  • Invitations: whoever invited you sees your name and the date in their Mini App profile; you see the people you invited the same way. The operator sees who invited whom, to understand how people find the service.
  • The operator sees aggregate statistics (how many users and workouts, by day and type), names of recent sign-ups, who invited whom and how many workouts each person has, to improve the service. Routes and coordinates are not shown in these statistics.

3. Why

To show you your route, stats and workout results, and, when you choose to, to the people you picked (section 2a). Data is not sold, not used for ads and not shared with third parties other than the services listed below, without which the bot can't work. The legal basis is your consent.

4. Services involved

  • Telegram — delivers messages and the live location (under its own privacy policy).
  • Cloudflare — network intermediary: the Mini App reaches the server through it. Technically it sees the IP address and requests.
  • OpenStreetMap — map tiles are loaded from OSM servers straight into your Telegram: they see your IP address and which part of the map is open. For a workout card the server loads tiles of the route area — OSM sees the server's IP address and the map area, without your Telegram ID or name.
  • unpkg.com and telegram.org — serve the Mini App scripts (the Leaflet map library and the Telegram SDK); they see your IP address.
  • Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) — after a workout the server sends it the coordinates of the route points, without your Telegram ID or name, to get the elevation. It sees the server's IP address, not yours.
  • OSRM at routing.openstreetmap.de (FOSSGIS e.V., Germany) — after a bike ride the server sends it the coordinates and times of the route points, without your Telegram ID or name, to snap the track to roads and measure the distance more precisely. It sees the server's IP address, not yours. If the service is unavailable, the ride is measured from the original track.

The database is stored on the operator's server; only the operator has access to it.

5. How long data is kept

  • Until you delete it yourself with /delete.
  • Database backups are made daily and kept for 7 days — after /delete your data disappears from them within 7 days at the latest.
  • Technical server logs (request times, errors) contain no coordinates and are overwritten when the service restarts.

6. Your rights

  • View your workouts — in the Mini App (“My workouts”).
  • Delete a single workout — on its screen in the Mini App.
  • Export all data about you to a JSON file — the /export command.
  • Correct your weight — the /weight command or the Mini App profile; language — the Mini App profile.
  • Delete all data and withdraw consent — the /delete command. Deletion happens immediately.
  • Messages in the chat with the bot (including live locations) are deleted with Telegram's own tools.
  • If you believe your rights have been violated, you can contact the data protection authority of your country.

7. Security

The Mini App receives data only after verifying the Telegram signature, so other people's workouts can't be seen without their action. The server isn't exposed to the internet directly, the bot runs under a separate account with minimal permissions, and deleted data is overwritten in the database file.

8. Terms of use

  • The service is provided “as is”, without warranties: recording may be interrupted; distance and calories are estimates, not medical advice.
  • Follow traffic rules. Don't look at your phone while riding.
  • You turn live location sharing on and off yourself; the bot does not track your location in the background.
  • SOS does not replace emergency services: delivery depends on Telegram and the mobile network. If you're in danger, call 112.
  • The operator may change these terms. The bot will then ask you to accept the new revision; until you do, new workouts are not recorded.
